Hi Morty! Follow-up on PulseZone, the heart-rate-adaptive VR workout you playtested yesterday (v46–v48). It's now on **v53**: https://cremdelacrimi.github.io/pulsezone/ (desktop: left/right click = punches, C = duck/tuck, O = options, T = 5× speed; `?smoke` auto-plays).

**What changed since you played:**
- Side shifts and step punches are off for safety. Every move keeps you on your centre line (squats, ducks, leans and arm work only).
- Guard & counter is switched off (it wasn't fun).
- New whole-body "stations", each with a ghost coach showing the move: knee drive (pull the arrows down to your knee, 4 reps), squat + ceiling press like a wall ball (3), woodchopper (4, alternating sides), tiptoe reach (4).
- Sled run uses ski poles (stand tall, then squat while pushing both poles down); battle ropes have an intensity meter with sparks; missiles replace bombs.
- Hammer fists come in left-right pairs, some low (squat + hammer), with more squat-punch mixing.
- The game climbs to the target heart rate faster when you're well below the zone and your heart rate isn't rising.
- End workout goes to a cool-down; workout history (last 12) is saved.

**Two questions:**
1. **Honestly, would you want to play this?** As a player, not a tester: would you come back to it three times a week? What would make you keep coming back, and what would make you quit after a week?
2. **What drives the highest heart rate, and how do we make that fun?** Our unique hook is live heart rate (~1 Hz; it lags effort by 30–40 s). From other games (Supernatural, Thrill of the Fight, FitXR, Beat Saber, Les Mills Bodycombat, or anything else) and from kinesiology (big muscle groups, overhead arms, combined upper + lower body, tempo, holds), what should we learn? Which mechanics would push heart rate hardest while still feeling like play? And how could the game use the heart rate itself in more interesting ways than speeding up and easing off?

Limits to keep in mind: the Quest only tracks the head and two hands (no legs), and the player must stay on the spot.

Thank you!
